The flags on the report, explained

A few items are marked high or low. Here is what each one actually means. Swipe to see all six.

flagged high

Transferrin saturation 49%

This measures iron travelling in the blood right now, and it jumps for hours after an iron dose. You had Hemfer the evening before, so this is the syrup showing up in the blood, not an overload. Ferritin of 42 confirms the stores are healthy. It does tell us twice-daily iron is now more than you need.

flagged high

Fasting glucose 106.67

This was not a true fasting sample. You had your usual pre-iron snack that morning, as planned, so the number does not count. The reliable sugar test is HbA1c, your 3-month average, which is 4.9 and excellent. Next time we will do it properly fasting.

flagged

Urine protein, trace

A trace amount in a concentrated morning sample, with normal kidneys and no blood or infection. Usually hydration or recent exercise. We will recheck with a well-hydrated morning sample. If it is still there, Dr. Parmar will take a look.

in range, low end

Vitamin C 0.56 and Vitamin A 0.42

Both normal but near the bottom of the range. This is a food job, not a medicine job. Winter citrus is perfectly timed: oranges, amla, guava and capsicum for C; carrots, pumpkin, sweet potato, papaya and greens with ghee for A.

high, and that is good

HDL cholesterol 76.67

HDL is the protective cholesterol. The lab flags anything above 60, but higher HDL lowers heart risk. The ghee, nuts and full-fat milk from the weight-gain plan are likely behind this. Total cholesterol 176 and LDL 77 are both healthy.

same as May

Bilirubin 1.46

Mildly raised in May (1.53) and mildly raised now, with every liver enzyme normal. This is the Gilbert's pattern, a harmless trait many people have. Nothing to treat.

Sleep, the next chapter

This report quietly answered a big question: the body causes of broken sleep are now mostly cleared. That narrows the search.

Ruled out

  • Thyroid. All normal.
  • Low haemoglobin. Normal now.
  • Empty iron stores. Ferritin 42 and rising toward 50.
  • Blood sugar. HbA1c 4.9, excellent.

Still on the list

  • Perimenopause. The commonest cause of waking at 2 to 4 AM at this stage, sometimes hot or sweaty.
  • The early-morning stress hormone, when the mind has been carrying a lot.
  • A warm room, late fluids, or a night-time sugar dip.

What to do now

  • Keep the 14-night sleep diary. Time you wake, and what you felt: hot, legs, bathroom, thoughts, or a dream.
  • Take the diary and this report to Dr. Nayna. She can judge whether the waking is hormonal, and whether the Restonin from January should restart.
  • Keep the bedtime routine. Magnesium, warm haldi milk with two dates, a cool room, Yog Nidra, most of your water before 7 PM.

Two small food jobs

For the two vitamins at the low end of normal. No tablets, just a little steering of the plate.

Vitamin C

Oranges, sweet lime, amla (fresh or murabba), guava, kiwi, capsicum, tomatoes. One citrus fruit a day through winter covers it.

Vitamin A

Carrots, pumpkin, sweet potato, papaya, methi, palak. Cook the greens with a spoon of ghee, because Vitamin A needs fat to be absorbed.
